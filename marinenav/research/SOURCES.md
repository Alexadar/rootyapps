# marinenav — oracle sources

Every source used, with URI, retrieval date, licence status, and the oracle IDs it backs.
Rule: no expected number enters a test corpus without a line in this file.

All sources below are **U.S. Government works in the public domain**, so every fixture is embedded
verbatim in the test targets (as Swift string constants — code, not SPM resources) and
`swift test` is green **offline on a fresh clone**. Verified: the Kits reference no `URLSession`,
no `Bundle.module`, no SPM `resources`, and import only `Foundation` (and `Testing` in tests).

### S2. Parker, *Tidal Analysis and Prediction*
- **Citation:** Bruce B. Parker, **NOAA Special Publication NOS CO-OPS 3**, Silver Spring MD,
  July 2007. LCCN 2007925298.
- **URI:** https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/50576/noaa_50576_DS1.pdf
  — retrieved **2026-07-26**, 19,440,828 bytes (has a text layer).
- **Licence:** U.S. Government work — **public domain**.
- **Backs:**
  - §3.4.2 eq. (3.1)/(3.3), pp. 91–94 — the prediction form
    `h(t) = H₀ + Σ fᵢHᵢ·cos(aᵢt + {V₀+u}ᵢ,Greenwich − κ′ᵢ)` → `Harmonics.height`, `Currents.velocity`.
  - eq. (3.2), p. 93 — `κ′ (= g) = κ + p·L − a·S/15`, `G = κ + p·L` → `Epoch`, oracle
    `parker-eq32-epoch-9414290`.
  - **NOAA operational convention** (pp. 41, 93–94): f and u are held **constant across each
    calendar year** (midyear value; Schureman Tables 14/15, extended to 2025 by Zetler 1982).
    `Nodal` evaluates them continuously, which Parker documents as the more accurate variant —
    recorded as a MODEL CAVEAT on the type.

## Geodesy

### S6. Vincenty (1975)
- **Citation:** T. Vincenty, "Direct and Inverse Solutions of Geodesics on the Ellipsoid with
  Application of Nested Equations", *Survey Review* XXIII/176 (1975), pp. 88–93. WGS-84.
- **Backs:** `vincenty_flinders_buninyong` (the published Flinders Peak ↔ Buninyong line).
  Note: the paper's α₂ = 127°10′25.07″ is the **back** azimuth; the Kit returns the forward
  azimuth at point 2, so the pinned value is +180°. That reinterpretation is recorded in the
  oracle's own `source` string.
- **Scoped claim:** Vincenty's *inverse* is documented not to converge for near-antipodal
  endpoints. The Kit reports `converged: false` rather than returning a plausible wrong number,
  and a test asserts that. The *direct* solution converges everywhere.

### S7. Karney, GeodTest

  UT 08ʰ24ᵐ41ˢ.
- **URI:** https://thenauticalalmanac.com/2024_Bowditch-_American_Practical_Navigator/Volume_2/09_Volume_2_Calculations_For_Navigation/Chapter_8_Sight_Reduction.pdf
  — retrieved **2026-07-26**, 693,210 bytes.
- **Licence:** U.S. Government work — **public domain**.
- **Backs:** `bowditch-805-kochab`. **This closes the `TODO(oracle):` that stood open through two
  earlier scaffold passes** — the sight-reduction pipeline previously had only trig identities
  behind it, never a published sight.
  Every intermediate quantity is pinned separately (dip, ha, refraction, Ho, LHA, Hc, Zn,
  intercept) so an error that cancels between two stages cannot hide.
- **Rounding note:** the almanac rounds each correction to 0.1′ *before applying it*. Carrying
  full precision therefore differs from the printed Ho by 0.072′ (dip 6.145′→6.1′, refraction

  printed values to **0.0000′**, showing the residual is the almanac's rounding, not our formulas.

## Findings that changed the implementation (2026-07-26)

Caught by fitting (A, G) back out of **NOAA's own predictions** and comparing against NOAA's
**own published constants** — a diagnostic that localises an error to a specific astronomical
term. Scripts: `scratch/tide_*.py`.

1. **Mean-longitude constants.** From-memory values were wrong: `s` by **+6.587°**, `h` by
   **+0.493°**. The diagnostic pointed at δs ≈ +6.2° (from M2 and O1) and δh ≈ +0.49° (from K1)
   *before* Table 1 was located; Table 1 then confirmed both exactly.
2. **ξ sign convention.** Three conventions are in circulation; direct-synthesis rms against NOAA
   was 0.383 / 0.115 / 0.080 m. **The best-fitting one was wrong** — it was compensating for the
   `s` error above. Schureman Table 6 settled it.
3. **`Qa` and `Ra` are inverses** (Schureman eq. 197 and 213): `f(M1) = f(O1)·(1/Qa)`,
   `f(L2) = f(M2)·(1/Ra)`. Using them the other way up made each fitted amplitude ~1.8× the
   published one — caught by the per-constituent diagnostic, independently on heights *and*
   currents.
4. **All 37 constituents are required.** An 8-constituent model leaves 0.060 m rms; the full set
   reaches 0.008 m.
5. **Consequence for testing:** every one of these bugs produced a smooth, plausible tide curve.
   A suite green against self-consistency would have shipped all of them.

### Achieved residuals — the evidence behind every tolerance

Synthesised from each station's published constants, compared against that station's published
predictions (`scratch/tide_full37.py`, `scratch/current_full37.py`):

| station | regime | window | rms | max | least-squares floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9414290 San Francisco | mixed semidiurnal | 4 yr hourly | **0.0076 m** | 0.0350 m | 0.0053 m |
| 8771450 Galveston | diurnal | 2 yr hourly | **0.0026 m** | 0.0098 m | 0.0021 m |
| 1612340 Honolulu | mixed, small range | 2 yr hourly | **0.0028 m** | 0.0102 m | 0.0019 m |
| 8443970 Boston | semidiurnal, 3 m | 2 yr hourly | **0.0088 m** | 0.0350 m | 0.0082 m |
| 9455920 Anchorage | 9 m, Cook Inlet | 2 yr hourly | 0.2218 m | 0.9985 m | 0.2212 m |
| 9414290 (feet) | — | 2 yr hourly | **0.0256 ft** | 0.0959 ft | 0.0170 ft |
| ACT1616 currents | rectilinear | 1 yr, 30-min | **1.185 cm/s** | 3.315 cm/s | 1.129 cm/s |

The "least-squares floor" is the best any 37-constituent model could do against that series.
Every row sits within ~0.6 mm (or 0.06 cm/s) of its floor, i.e. **the synthesis is at the model's
theoretical best** and the remaining error is published-constant rounding — not convention error.

**Anchorage is the honest exception and a real MODEL CAVEAT:** our synthesis is 0.6 mm from the
optimal 37-constituent fit, yet still 0.22 m rms from NOAA. NOAA's Cook Inlet predictions use more
constituents than it publishes in `harcon`, so *no* 37-constituent synthesis can reproduce them.
This is a property of the station, documented on `Harmonics`, not a defect to be tuned away.

### Remaining known imperfections (stated, not hidden)

- **M1, S1, R2** retain per-constituent phase offsets of ~20°, ~19° and ~41° against NOAA at
  9414290. Every discrete alternative convention was tested (`scratch/tide_variants.py`) and all
  made the overall residual **worse**, so the current rules are the best available. Combined these
  three carry < 0.02 m of amplitude, inside the stated tolerance. Likely cause: NOAA infers rather
  than analyses these minor constituents. **Not resolved.**
- ~~Schureman values read from OCR~~ — **CLOSED 2026-07-28.** Both tables were re-read directly
  from the NOAA scan images. Table 6: **25/25 values exact**. Table 1: all five mean-longitude
  constants confirmed *twice over* — the scan's century-year table (book p.163) gives the elements
  at **1900 Jan 1.0**, exactly half a day after the formulas' epoch (Greenwich mean noon,
  1899 Dec 31). Advancing each formula constant by half a day of its own tabulated rate reproduces
  the published century-year value:

  | element | formula constant (epoch 1899 Dec 31.5) | + ½ day × rate | Table 1 at 1900 Jan 1.0 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | h  | 279.696678 | 280.1895 | **280.190** ✓ |
  | s  | 270.437422 | 277.0254 | **277.026** ✓ |
  | p  | 334.328019 | 334.3837 | **334.384** ✓ |
  | N  | 259.182533 | 259.1561 | **259.156** ✓ |
  | p₁ | 281.220833 | 281.2209 | **281.221** ✓ |

  This also explains the original bug precisely: **277.0248 and 280.1895 were never wrong numbers**
  — they are the *1900 Jan 1.0* values. The error was pairing them with a T reckoned from
  1899 Dec 31.5, half a day earlier. The scan's rate columns confirm the linear terms too
  (Moon 1336ʳ + 307.892°/century = 481,267.892; node −5ʳ − 134.142 = −1934.142; and
  "Revolution of moon's node, 360°/N = 18.61 Julian years", which the Kit already asserts).
